Strategic positioning

PositioningDesigning the offering and its image so it occupies a distinct, valued place in the target customer's mind, relative to competitors (Walker & Mullins; Jooste et al.).

Three positioning concepts

Memory hook · positioning concepts
"FSE"
  • Functional — solves a problem (cavity prevention in toothpaste)
  • Symbolic — self-enhancement / status (Rolex, Louis Vuitton)
  • Experiential — sensory/cognitive pleasure (driving a BMW)
Exam linkUsing two or more concepts at once tends to confuse buyers and weakens positioning. J&J positions each brand separately; Nike uses its corporate brand to position all product lines — know why each fits its concept.

Four faulty-positioning errors

Memory hook · faulty positioning
"U · O · C · D"
  • Under — customers have only a vague, undistinctive idea of the brand
  • Over — customers' view is too narrow (unaware of other products)
  • Confused — frequent, contradictory messages
  • Doubtful — claims aren't seen as credible

Marketing metrics

Metrics quantify, compare and interpret marketing performance — don't confuse them with financial metrics (gross margin, sales growth) that come straight from financial reports.

CategoryWhat it captures
ObservableBehaviour: purchases, CLTV, acquisition, retention, cross-sell, leads
UnobservableAttitudes (via surveys): satisfaction, service quality, perceived value, loyalty
ExternalStanding vs competitors (e.g. reported market-share statistics)
BudgetSpend as % of turnover vs benchmarks (~3–10%, up to 20% for a launch)

Key formulas

  • Unit market share = unit sales ÷ total market units ×100
  • Response rate = responses ÷ promotions sent ×100
  • Retention rate = retained ÷ total customers ×100
  • Acquisition cost = acquisition spend ÷ new customers

Share of mind vs heart

Knowing ≠ liking. Coca-Cola's share-of-mind may be 60% but only 35% prefer it — its share of heart is lower. Awareness → interest → desire → action.

Practical example — Peter's Print ShopStarted with 532 customers, lost 96. Retention rate = (532−96)/532 = 82% (within the typical 70–90%). Retention cost = R532,000 ÷ 436 ≈ R1,220/customer. If retention costs rise, segment by value and keep the most growable customers.

Advanced metrics, CLTV & the dashboard

Memory hook · advanced metrics
"D-B-C-C"
  • Demand forecasts — econometric modelling
  • Brand equity — Aaker / Keller models
  • CLTV — customer lifetime value (NPV of future profit)
  • Customer equity — sum of all customers' CLTVs
Practical example — Starbucks LTVStarbucks isn't selling a $5.90 coffee — it's acquiring a ~$15,000 customer. It combines three LTV equations (simple ≈$25,272; custom ≈$5,489; traditional ≈$11,535; average ≈$14,099), which justifies investing in Wi-Fi, comfy couches and other benefits to raise retention and margin.

A marketing dashboard (e.g. ThyssenKrupp) consolidates financial and non-financial, short- and long-term metrics across customer value, retention, acquisition, brand equity, innovation and internal fitness — reviewed weekly/quarterly.

Strategic relationships

Memory hook · relationship types
"I-E-S-A-J"
  • Intermediate-customer — wholesalers, retailers, franchises
  • End-user — co-creation with customers
  • Strategic customers — dedicated teams (P&G's 200-person Walmart team)
  • Alliances — cooperative agreement (P&G ↔ Google)
  • Joint ventures — a new entity (Coca-Cola ↔ Nestlé)

Reasons to form relationships: develop new markets, compete in mature markets, cut costs, leverage strengths, and reduce risk. "Tribes" are active co-creators of brand value.

The marketing mix (4Ps)

Product

Position the product/brand vs competitors and manage the product life cycle. Moon (2005): re-position to push a product back into growth — reverse positioning (strip expected attributes, add surprising ones), breakaway positioning, and stealth positioning.

Price

Three orientations: cost (breakeven, cost-plus), competition (price leader, sealed bid, reverse auction), demand (what buyers will pay). Tactics: single/flexible price, price lining, leader, bait, odd-even, bundle, two-part, dynamic. Mind global pricing ethics.

Place / distribution

The value-chain strategy: which channels, the role of channel members, and the support provided. Aligns with positioning (e.g. Samsung pulled out of discount chains into specialty stores).

Promotion

ATL = paid mass media (wide reach). BTL = direct, controllable, one-to-one (sampling, personal selling). TTL = integrated ATL+BTL (digital blurs them).

Promotion: IMC & AIDA

Integrated Marketing CommunicationsA plan that evaluates the strategic roles of different communication disciplines and combines them for clarity, consistency and maximum impact (American Assoc. of Advertising Agencies).

Personal selling matters most when…

  • High-value or custom-made product
  • Few customers
  • Technically complex
  • Customers geographically concentrated

Advertising & sales promotion when…

  • Low-value, standardised product
  • Many customers
  • Simple to understand
  • Customers geographically dispersed

The annual marketing plan

The plan is a road map and a control tool. Learn its six sections (Cravens & Piercy).

Memory hook · marketing-plan sections
"SMOMFC"
  • Strategic situation summary (external/internal environment)
  • Market-target description (segments, profiles, size, competitors, positioning guide)
  • Objectives for the target(s) — SMART, with measures
  • Marketing-programme positioning strategy (product, place, price, promotion, research, coordination)
  • Forecasts & budgets
  • Control — marketing-driven implementation & control
Exam linkObjectives must be SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound). Include contingency plans; prioritise resources when serving two or more targets.

Section key points

  • Positioning runs concept → strategy → effectiveness; the concept is functional, symbolic or experiential.
  • Metrics quantify marketing performance; advanced metrics (demand forecast, brand equity, CLTV, customer equity) project long-term value.
  • Strategic relationships range from intermediate-customer to joint ventures; they develop markets, cut cost and reduce risk.
  • The 4Ps are coordinated to deliver the positioning concept; IMC + AIDA structure the promotion mix.
  • The marketing plan has six sections with SMART objectives — a forecast and control tool.
