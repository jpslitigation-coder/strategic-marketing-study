Roles & responsibilities

The sales function (Barrett)

  • One-to-one; where the brand comes to life
  • Relationship-driven; looks after individuals — can't be averaged
  • Shifts from price/discount to total cost of ownership
  • Positions the firm as a risk-free alternative

The advertising agency

  • Agency director — strategy with the client
  • Creative team — design, film, copy
  • Market researchers — audits & surveys
  • Media planners — best media match

Measured on impact — changing behaviour within time & budget (recall/recognition tests, attitude, sales).

The Chief Marketing Officer: seven tasks

Memory hook · the CMO's 7 tasks
"Really Very Smart Chiefs Run Top Scorecards"
  • Role — establish marketing's role in the firm
  • Voice — own the voice of the market
  • Strategy — responsibility for marketing strategy
  • Coordinate — with other areas of the firm
  • Run — run the marketing organisation
  • Transform — lead the marketing transformation effort
  • Scorecard — establish a marketing scorecard & metrics
Practical example — H&M fitH&M sells "cheap chic" and runs on frugality internally (execs rarely fly business, cabs frowned upon). The fit between strategy/positioning and internal culture/management helps explain its success — strategy must align with structure, culture and systems.

The strategic marketing audit

Marketing auditA process to evaluate a system by comparing results with expectations — so a set of expectations must exist first. It's a reflective tool covering mission/objectives, business composition, market analysis and the CMO's role.

Cravens & Piercy's generic audit works through: corporate mission & objectives → business-composition strategies → market analysis → and the CMO's role in planning, implementation and measurement.

Implementation: two factor-sets

The marketing plan identifies who implements each activity. Effective planners aren't always effective implementers — match people to tasks.

Formulating a marketing strategy

Broad steps: snapshot of the current situation → define the target audience → list goals → develop communication strategies/tactics → set the budget. For digital, Stokes gives a five-part framework.

Memory hook · Stokes' digital framework
"C-V-O-T-O"
  • Context — situation analysis (company, customers, competitors, environment)
  • Value exchange — what value you add & how you'll know you succeeded
  • Objectives — SMART
  • Tactics & evaluation — fit tactics to objectives (SEO for retention, social for awareness)
  • Ongoing optimisation — how fast & flexibly you can adapt
Exam link"The mark of a good marketing strategy is not how many gadgets and neologisms are crammed in, but how effectively it achieves worthy goals" (Satell). Evaluate frameworks — don't just describe them.

Case study — Uber leads an industry's disruption

In 2010 Uber introduced booking a car on a smartphone and tracking it live, with tiered offers (UberX, Pool, Black, SUV, WAV, and concepts like UberAir). It is a P2P model that reconfigured the taxi market space rather than competing inside the traditional box.

  • Disruption: makes getting a ride as easy as tapping a phone; raised capital from Google and others.
  • Resistance: blocked/banned in several markets — incumbents and regulators push back on disruptors.
  • Marketer's job: find profit in the reconfigured space, leverage data & CRM, manage reputation, and keep optimising.
Connect it backUber ties Section 5 to the whole module: strategic space & drift (3.4), digital/P2P (3.2), positioning & the mix (3.5), and ethical/regulatory tension (3.3).

Section key points

  • Sales is one-to-one and relationship-driven; the ad agency is measured on behaviour change within time & budget.
  • The CMO has seven tasks, from owning the voice of the market to establishing a scorecard.
  • A marketing audit compares results with expectations — expectations must exist first.
  • Implementation hinges on structural and behavioural factors; planners ≠ implementers.
  • Stokes' digital framework: context, value exchange, SMART objectives, tactics/evaluation, ongoing optimisation.
  • Uber shows how a disruptor reconfigures market space — rewarding market sensing and agility.
