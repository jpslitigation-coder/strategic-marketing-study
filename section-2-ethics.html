Ethics & CSR in Marketing

Doing the right thing is not just moral — done well, it becomes brand image, market share and trust.

Three definitions to keep straight

EthicsThe study of the proper course of action — right and wrong in human behaviour. It answers, "What do I do?" Without it, actions would be haphazard and aimless.
Marketing ethicsThe application of moral principles and values in marketing — across advertising, promotion and pricing (Vitell; Sultana et al., 2016).
Ethical marketingA philosophy of honesty, fairness and responsibility. Right and wrong are partly subjective, so firms set guidelines to ensure intent is clear and achieved.
Exam linkExaminers reward candidates who distinguish these three and then apply them. Don't blur "ethics" with "ethical marketing".

Principles of ethical marketing

Memory hook · ethical-marketing principles
"TRUE-P"
  • Truth — a shared standard of truth in communications
  • Real advertising — a clear distinction between advertising and sensationalism
  • Unhidden endorsements — endorsements are clear and transparent
  • Every privacy protected — consumer privacy maintained at all times
  • Policy compliance — government standards & regulations adhered to

Socially responsible organisations & CSR

An organisation is socially responsible when it shows concern for the people, society and environment where it does business — acting morally to create a positive impact on its stakeholders (employees, community, consumers, shareholders).

CSRAn approach where a company (a) recognises its activities have a wide impact on society and development, and (b) actively manages its economic, social, environmental and human-rights responsibilities (Tripathi & Singh, 2014).

Because consumers have the power to decide which companies succeed or fail, marketers carry a heavy responsibility to act humanely. Increasingly, people want a company's social/environmental record before they buy from, invest in or work for it.

Memory hook · CSR concern
"PSE"
  • People — employees, consumers, community
  • Society & development
  • Environment — where the business operates

Marketers and ethical conflict

Ethical conflict arises because the company, the industry and society all have diverse needs — and because an individual's personal values can clash with the organisation.

Practical example — tobaccoCigarette marketing is profitable and employs thousands (good for the economy → industry/company interest). But documented harm to health makes it an ethical conflict against society's interest. Likewise, promoting a false "cure" for personal profit breaks the rules of marketing ethics.

Areas of social concern (Table 7)

Tripathi & Singh flag five recurring areas where marketing must tread carefully.

Memory hook · five areas of concern
"N-R-G-T-C"
  • Natural environment — resources, energy, waste, pollution, packaging
  • Relationship marketing — what conduct/rules govern the buyer–seller bond?
  • Green marketing — Reduce, Reuse, Recycle; ecologically-safe products
  • Targeting children — adverts without parental consent are unethical
  • Cause-related marketing — must be transparent with a genuine company–cause fit

Green marketing vs cause-related marketing

Green marketing

Developing and distributing ecologically-safe products/packaging that are (1) less toxic, (2) more durable, (3) contain reusable materials, (4) are recyclable.

ExampleIn Germany & Canada, P&G found consumers strongly preferred refill pouches of detergent over discarding large plastic bottles.

Cause-related vs social marketing

Cause-related marketing mainly helps the business — it must be transparent with a good company–cause fit, or it looks like exploiting a charity. Social marketing isn't tied to a company; it exists only to address a social problem.

Good fitA bottled-water brand supporting a clean-water cause in Africa/Asia.

Deceptive advertising — how to critique one

  • Why is it not truthful?
  • What approach/appeal does it use?
  • What fraudulent or misleading techniques appear?

Why ethics pays (the assessable point)

Ethics doesn't automatically raise profit — but a responsible firm gains durable advantages, provided its strategy is genuinely ethically compliant.

Brand & market

Excellent brand image and increased market share.

Costs & people

Lower operating costs; easier to attract & retain employees.

Investors & trust

Attracts investors; privacy/transparency becomes a competitive edge.

Exam link · LO 5Balance the benefits with the tension: communicating CSR invites scrutiny — the more committed a firm is, the more dilemmas can arise (e.g. Green Mountain Coffee). A strong answer evaluates, it doesn't just list.

Section key points

  • Marketing ethics = applying moral principles/values across advertising, promotion and pricing.
  • An organisation is socially responsible when it shows concern for people, society and the environment where it operates.
  • Ethical conflict stems from the differing needs of company, industry and society (and personal values vs the organisation).
  • Cause-related marketing must be distinguished from social marketing and needs a genuine, transparent company–cause fit.
  • Ethics won't automatically raise profit, but responsible firms build trust, reputation and long-term value.
