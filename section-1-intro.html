What is strategic marketing?

Strategic marketing is the latest stage in the evolution of marketing philosophy. It is about long-term, integrated decision-making on products, pricing, promotion and distribution across multiple (even global) markets, and the ability to "innovate, discover and break rules to create sustainable value for customers" (Jooste et al., 2012).

Market-driven strategyAn approach in which the market and the customer are the starting point for business strategy. Because you understand the market and customers first, you can build competitive advantage and long-term relationships (Cravens & Piercy, 2013).

Many firms think they are customer-driven but are really product- and sales-driven. Becoming truly market-driven means (1) a company-wide passion for customers, (2) organising around customer segments instead of products, and (3) understanding customers through qualitative and quantitative research (Kotler & Keller).

Memory hook · drivers of strategic marketing
"FRESH CDG"
  • Fragmentation of markets (customisation)
  • Rapid technological innovation (e.g. Uber)
  • Environmental & climate change
  • Shortening product life cycles
  • Higher demand for superior value
  • Competition — the battle for market share
  • Demographic shifts
  • Globalisation & integrated value offers / new competitors

What makes a firm market-driven?

Wharton's George Day says market-driven organisations excel at three distinctive capabilities. Opportunities and threats often begin as weak signals from the periphery of a business, so firms must build peripheral vision.

Four characteristics of a market-driven strategy

Cravens & Piercy identify four characteristics (Table 1). Learn them as a chain that always ends in superior customer value.

Memory hook · characteristics
"CD-MS"
  • Customer focus — understand needs, competitors, and get all functions working together
  • Distinctive capabilities — processes, skills/knowledge, coordination, assets
  • Match customer value with organisational capabilities (+ leverage technology)
  • Superior performance — deliver superior value (differentiation, lower price, or both)
Practical example — leverage technologyA market-driven firm treats emerging technology as a way to leapfrog competitors. Samsung poured ~6% of revenue into R&D on LCDs, chipsets and mobile telephony to jump ahead of analogue-era rivals like Sony — turning technology into distinctive capability, not just a cost.

The strategic marketing process

Put organisational strategy, culture and systems at the centre. Marketing then runs three moves. For the whole thing to work, strategy, structure, systems, culture and people must operate synergistically.

Memory hook · the three moves
"U · S · I"
  • Understand markets & segments (Section 3.4)
  • Select market-driven strategies (Section 3.5)
  • Implement market-driven strategies (Section 3.6)
Exam linkThis three-move spine is the backbone of the whole module. If a question asks you to "design and manage a market-driven strategy", structure your answer as Understand → Select → Implement.

Production vs market orientation

Walker & Mullins argue a market orientation is crucial for global success. The difference shows up in every business function.

FunctionProduction orientationMarket orientation
ProductSells what it can make; focus on cost & functionMakes what it can sell; focus on needs & opportunities
Product lineNarrowBroad
PricingBased on production/distribution costBased on perceived benefits
ResearchTechnical; product improvement & cost-cuttingMarket research; new opportunities & tech to satisfy needs
PackagingProtects product; minimise costCustomer convenience; a promotional tool
PromotionEmphasis on features, quality, priceEmphasis on benefits & solving customer problems
Practical example — the Apple nuanceApple shows the limit of "just ask customers". Jobs avoided most focus groups: "customers don't know what they want until you show them." Apple builds few products then uses marketing to pull customers in. Lesson: get closer than ever to customers — close enough to tell them what they'll need before they realise it.

The new era: globalisation, services, IT & e-commerce

Three forces reshape strategy: globalisation (adapting products/programmes to many contexts), the growth of services (two-thirds of US economic activity), and information technology. Social platforms give rich data to define and serve segments.

The e-commerce quadrants (+ a 5th way)

Digital marketing = 3 things

  • A powerful communication platform
  • A direct sales platform
  • A source for data mining & management

…and it exposes you to word of mouth that can bolster or damage your brand.

Data-mining principles (ICO)

  • Justify collection (match customer needs)
  • Keep it secure
  • Protect from inappropriate disclosure
  • Be open about how data is used
Exam link · LO 6 (MIS)Privacy is becoming a competitive advantage — even just talking about privacy is. Tie digital data (MIS) back to trust, segmentation and personalisation.

Social media & mobile marketing

Social networks are defined by social interactivity — they are places to listen, interact and collaborate, not just push products. This gives rise to "conversation marketing". Blanchard's model keeps social effort anchored in business objectives.

Practical example — continuity beats campaignsSusan's conference launches a hashtag #Leadership2024 in June, runs live Q&As, integrates it across all materials, lets remote attendees join on the day, and keeps the conversation alive after the event with downloadable resources that capture emails. Traditional campaigns have a lifespan; social gives continuity.

Keywords are the firm's DNA

Essential for SEO, profiles, news feeds, website content, tweets, YouTube tags, PPC and community-building. Use analytics to find the terms people actually search.

Mobile: 5 compliance mistakes to avoid

  • Ignoring opt-in / opt-out rules
  • Deceptive advertising
  • Violating COPPA (children's privacy)
  • Phishing / SMiShing
  • Skipping legal review of copy & policies

Section key points

  • Organisational strategy drives structure, culture and systems.
  • The objective of marketing strategy is to coordinate resources and activities to hit organisational objectives within chosen product/service markets.
  • The marketing plan spells out what, why, who, when, where and how — a forecast and measurement tool.
  • Globalisation, growing services and IT force firms to adapt strategies, programmes and products to many contexts.
  • Social platforms provide rich data to define and customise offers to segments — and expose brands to word of mouth.
  • Digital marketing carries legal implications: data-protection law governs collection and use of personal data.
